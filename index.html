Remember the first computer you used? The buttons you clicked and the keyboard you learned on, now under your thumbs, in the apps you already type in.

The joy of it

  • Six eras of buttonsOne-bit pixels, bevelled grey, glossy gel, raised and letterpressed, frosted and flat, plump and rounded. Swipe the keys to travel between them.
  • The sound of the real thingEvery era plays keystrokes recorded from a real keyboard of its time. Keys on the left sound left, and keys on the right sound right.
  • Pressed the way they were1984 buttons invert, 1997 bevels flip, the 2002 return key breathes like a default button waiting for you, and 2020 keys give a little squeeze.
  • Felt in your thumbsA soft tap of haptics on each press, so typing stops feeling like tapping on glass.
  • Installed the old wayUnlocking the eras is half the fun. 1997 arrives on a floppy disk, 2002 on a CD, 2011 as a download, and by 2014 it installs itself.
  • A time machine in your pocketPress the buttons in the app, just to hear each year again.

The essentials

  • Stays on your iPhoneWhat you type isn’t sent anywhere. The keyboard has no networking code, accounts, ads or analytics.
  • Pay once, keep for good1984 and 2020 are free. $3.99 once unlocks the other four, and any eras added later. No subscription.
  • Try before you buyPress every era’s buttons and hear its sound in the app before you pay for anything.
  • English and 日本語Romaji to kanji conversion runs on your iPhone, so it works offline too.
  • Still a smart keyboardAutocorrect, suggestions and an emoji panel, so you give up nothing for the nostalgia.
  • In the apps you already useAdd it once and it’s there for your messages, notes and mail.

Sound, haptics and paid eras need iOS’s Allow Full Access. Typing with the free eras works without it, and iOS uses its own keyboard for passwords. How your privacy is protected. Keyboard sounds are cut from recordings shared on Freesound: sound credits.
